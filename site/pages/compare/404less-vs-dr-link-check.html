404less vs Dr. Link Check.

Dr. Link Check is a crawler you point at a start URL. It looks for broken links, blacklisted targets, and pages that return 200 while reading like an error. 404less is the 404 log on your own site, plus a crawler, plus a redirect you can paste.

404less has no subscription. MIT, on your Cloudflare account. A crawl counts pages discovered, default 100 per run, not “unique resources” in their sense. See /self-host for the free-plan arithmetic in the README.

What the crawl checks

The homepage (drlinkcheck.com) says the bot starts at a URL and walks HTML and CSS. It checks internal links, outbound links, images, stylesheets, and other resource files. Each link goes through:

  • A broken-link check: URL shape, response time, SSL certificate, and an error status such as 404 or 500.
  • A blacklist check for malicious content.
  • A soft-error check: HTTP 200, with page content that indicates something is wrong.

The report goes from a summary down to the location of the link in the code. Results can be sorted, filtered, and exported to CSV. Schedules on that page are monthly, weekly, or daily, with email. You can include or exclude URLs, limit crawl speed, and choose who gets the email.

The homepage and pricing page do not describe self-hosting, a script on your 404 template, referrers, or a generated _redirects / vercel.json rule.

Soft errors and visitor hits

The soft-error check is a real difference, and it is in their favour on the pages read. 404less’s crawler treats every 2xx and 3xx as a working link (isWorking in apps/web/src/server/api/crawl/checker.ts), plus 401, 429, and any status you configured to ignore. A 200 that says “page not found” in the body is a success to that function. The way 404less sees that class of URL is the embed, and only when the page is detected as a 404 (title, data-404, or the status metas). A soft 404 that is your homepage served for a missing path does not run the embed. That case is the Search Console guide.

The other direction: a URL that nothing links to, which people still request because of an old tweet or a typed path, is invisible to a crawler that only follows links. The embed records it, with the referrer when the browser sent one. Dr. Link Check’s homepage describes a crawler, so that URL is outside the job they document there.

They also check blacklists and SSL on the targets. 404less does not. If that is the report you need, their product is the one whose homepage lists it.

A practical split

Dr. Link Check fits a scheduled audit of a site you might not be deploying: outbound links, assets, a blacklist, a soft-error pass, a CSV. The $0 row is two sites and 1,500 links, one-time, with the caveats in the table.

404less fits the site you can edit. The 404 template gets the script, the dashboard gets the hits, the link detail page gets a rule for the host you use. Setup is the deploy guide and the embed.
