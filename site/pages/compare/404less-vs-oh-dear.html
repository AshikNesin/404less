404less vs Oh Dear.

Oh Dear watches whether a site is up, certified, resolving, and free of broken links, from outside the site. 404less is the list of 404s and a redirect you can paste, on a Worker you run.

Prices they publish

404less has no plan table. MIT license, deployed to your Cloudflare account. Defaults on /self-host: 5 sites per team, 100 pages per crawl unless you raise it (max 500), digests via Slack, Resend, or Pushover.

Scope

Oh Dear’s homepage describes a monitor you do not install: uptime checks every minute from more than one location, broken-link crawls and Lighthouse daily, alerts when a certificate is near expiry, a cron did not run, a link breaks, or a Lighthouse score drops. Alert destinations named there: Slack, email, Teams, PagerDuty, webhook. Status pages can carry your logo and domain. Data is described as hosted in the EU.

404less does none of the uptime, certificate, DNS, cron, Lighthouse, or status-page work. It records 404 hits from a script on your 404 page, crawls links and assets on a schedule, suggests a replacement URL, copies a redirect rule, and exposes a read-only MCP server. Digests go to Slack, email, or Pushover.

If the question is “page the human when the site is down,” Oh Dear is the product whose homepage is about that. If the question is “which missing URLs did people request, and what redirect do I paste,” that is the 404less list.

Broken links, specifically

Oh Dear’s homepage FAQ says broken-link crawls run daily, from the outside, the way a visitor’s browser would, with no agent to install. Application health checks call an endpoint you expose. The pricing page lists “broken links & mixed content” on every plan.

Those pages do not describe a log of the 404 responses your own site returned to visitors, a per-platform redirect snippet, or an MCP server. That is an absence on the two pages read for this draft, not a claim that no Oh Dear document anywhere mentions them.

[ASHIK: skim their broken-link feature page and their docs search for “404 log”, “redirect”, and “MCP”. If any of those exist, add the URL here and correct the paragraph above.]

404less finds broken links two ways. The crawler fetches pages and checks links. The embed records a visitor who landed on the 404 template, which catches URLs that are no longer linked from anywhere you crawl. Suggestions need a sitemap import or a crawl first. The crawler treats any 2xx or 3xx as working, so a soft 404 (a 200 that looks like an error) is not flagged. Oh Dear’s pages use the phrase “broken links” and do not, on the pages read here, define a soft-404 check. Dr. Link Check does; that comparison is separate.

A practical split

An agency watching client sites for downtime, certificates, and a daily link crawl, with a status page in the agency’s brand, is who the Oh Dear pricing page is written for. The entry price on that page is €49 a month for 10 sites, before VAT.

A person with a Hugo or Next site on Cloudflare Pages, who wants the 404s and a _redirects line, is who the Pages guide is written for. The two products do not replace each other.
